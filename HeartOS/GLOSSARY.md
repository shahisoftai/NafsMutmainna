# Glossary

> Islamic terms used throughout the Heart OS documentation. Each term is a short, working definition — enough to understand the docs, not a full fiqh treatise. For deeper study, consult a scholar.

---

## A

### Adhkar (أذكار)
The plural of *dhikr*. Remembrances of Allah — short phrases (SubhanAllah, Alhamdulillah, La ilaha illa Allah, Allahu Akbar) recited at specific times (morning, evening, after Salah, before sleep). The most famous collection is *Hisn al-Muslim* (The Fortress of the Muslim).

### Akhlaq (أخلاق)
Character, manners, moral conduct. In the Quran, the Prophet ﷺ is described as being on the highest of akhlaq (Surah Al-Qalam 68:4). The 200 attributes include both the akhlaq (Character) domain and the Diseases of Heart domain, which are the *negatives* of akhlaq.

### Al-Qalb (القلب)
The heart. In the Islamic tradition, the qalb is the *spiritual centre* of the human being — not merely the organ that pumps blood, but the seat of intellect, will, and spiritual perception. Heart OS is an *operating system* for the qalb.

### Ammarah (النفس الأمّارة)
"The soul that commands evil." The lowest of the four Nafs states (see `01_core_tables/nafs_states.md`). The base-desire-driven self. See also *Lawwamah*, *Mulhamah*, *Mutmainnah*.

### Amradh al-Qalb (أمراض القلب)
"Diseases of the heart." The classical Islamic category of spiritual diseases — Riya, Kibr, Hasad, Nifaq, Ujb, Hiqd, Tama, and the other negative attributes in `07_appendices/200_attributes.md`.

---

## B

### Bidi'ah (بدعة)
Innovation in religious matters. What is not in the Quran or Sunnah is not automatically *haram*, but is not automatically *recommended* either. The Heart OS recommendations are all *traditional* — Quran, Hadith, established duas — never *new* practices.

---

## D

### Dhikr (ذكر)
Remembrance of Allah. The most fundamental act of worship after the five pillars. The Heart OS shows a *dhikr card* on the Intervention screen for every check-in.

### Dhann (ظن)
Suspicion, conjecture. The Quran distinguishes between *hasan al-dhann* (good opinion) and *sayyi' al-dhann* (bad opinion). Excessive suspicion is one of the 200 attributes (negative).

### Dua (دعاء)
Supplication, prayer, call upon Allah. Distinct from the formal *salah* (the ritual prayer). Heart OS shows both Quranic duas (verses that are themselves supplications, like *"Rabbana atina fid-dunya hasanah"*) and Prophetic duas (supplications the Prophet ﷺ taught).

---

## F

### Fard (فرض)
An obligatory act. The five daily prayers are *fard*. The Heart OS user is reminded of *fard* through the habit tracker (Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha) but the app does not check or enforce — it is a companion, not a muhtasib.

### Fiqh (فقه)
Jurisprudence. The *human* derivation of rulings from Quran and Sunnah. Heart OS does not provide fiqh rulings; it provides *guidance* (Quran, Hadith, dua, dhikr, action). For specific rulings, the user should consult a qualified scholar.

---

## G

### Ghadab (غضب)
Anger. The classical starting point of the *Anger → Mercy* pathway. The Quran distinguishes between *makhmudah* (praiseworthy) anger — for the sake of Allah — and *madhmumah* (blameworthy) anger — from ego.

### Gheebah (غيبة)
Backbiting — speaking ill of a person in their absence. One of the 200 negative attributes. The classical hadith compares it to eating one's dead brother's flesh (Surah Al-Hujurat 49:12).

### Ghibtah (غبطة)
The *praiseworthy* form of "envy" — wishing for a blessing someone else has, without wanting it taken from them. Distinct from *hasad* (envy proper), which is the negative attribute.

### Ghaflah (غفلة)
Heedlessness, forgetfulness (of Allah). One of the 200 negative attributes. The starting point of the *Heedlessness → Presence* pathway.

---

## H

### Hadith (حديث)
A recorded saying, action, or approval of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. The *second* source of Islamic law after the Quran. Heart OS cites Hadith for almost every recommendation. The most authentic collections are *Sahih al-Bukhari* and *Sahih Muslim*.

### Hasad (حسد)
Envy proper — disliking a blessing someone else has and wishing it taken away. The classical starting point of the *Envy → Contentment* pathway. The Quran commands refuge from the evil of the envier (Surah Al-Falaq 113:5).

### Hikmah (حكمة)
Wisdom. One of the 200 positive attributes. The Quran pairs *kitab* (book) with *hikmah* in several verses.

### Hilm (حلم)
Forbearance, calm in the face of provocation. The classical second step of the *Anger → Mercy* pathway. The opposite of *ghadab*.

---

## I

### Ihsan (إحسان)
"To worship Allah as if you see Him; for if you cannot see Him, He sees you." The highest station of worship, defined by the Prophet ﷺ in the *Hadith of Jibril* (Sahih Muslim 8). The terminal step of the *Heedlessness → Presence* pathway.

### Ikhlas (إخلاص)
Sincerity, purity of intention. The terminal step of the *Pride → Humility* pathway. The opposite of *riya* (showing off).

### Ilm (علم)
Knowledge — specifically, knowledge of Allah, His names, His commands, and His creation. The starting point of the *Knowledge → Nearness* pathway. The Quran repeatedly commands the seeking of *ilm*.

### Iman (إيمان)
Faith, belief. The first of the 10 domains. Distinct from *Islam* (the outward religion) and *Ihsan* (the highest worship). The Quran says *iman* increases with obedience and decreases with sin.

### Inabah (إنابة)
Turning back to Allah — the conscious, deliberate reorientation after *tawbah*. The second step of the *Sin → Love* pathway.

### Istighfar (استغفار)
Seeking forgiveness. The verbal form is *astaghfirullah* ("I seek forgiveness from Allah"). A central practice of the *Lawwamah* state.

---

## J

### Jannah (جنة)
Paradise. The Quran describes multiple levels. *Jannat al-Firdaws* is the highest. Heart OS does not depict Paradise — it focuses on the journey of the heart.

### Jahl (جهل)
Ignorance. One of the 200 negative attributes. The Quran distinguishes *jahl* from *qillat al-ilm* — the former is a *choice* to remain uninformed; the latter is simply not having learned.

---

## K

### Kibr (كبر)
Arrogance, pride. The starting point of the *Pride → Humility* pathway. The Prophet ﷺ said: "He who has the weight of a mustard seed of kibr in his heart will not enter Paradise." (Sahih Muslim 91)

---

## L

### Lawwamah (النفس اللوّامة)
"The self-reproaching soul." The second of the four Nafs states. The believer's *normal* state — the conscience is active, sins are regretted. The default baseline in Heart OS.

---

## M

### Mahabbah (محبة)
Love, especially the love of Allah. The terminal step of the *Sin → Love* and *Knowledge → Nearness* pathways. The highest spiritual station in v1 of Heart OS.

### Muraqabah (مراقبة)
Spiritual watchfulness, the awareness that Allah is watching. The third step of the *Heedlessness → Presence* pathway.

### Mulhamah (النفس الملهمة)
"The inspired soul." The third of the four Nafs states. The soul that has been *inspired* to distinguish good from evil. It receives *ilham* from Allah.

### Mutmainnah (النفس المطمئنة)
"The tranquil soul." The fourth and highest of the four Nafs states. The soul at peace with Allah, with His decree, and with His creation. The state of the Siddiqin (the truthful).

---

## N

### Nafs (نفس)
The self, the soul, the ego. The Quran uses *nafs* in many senses. In Heart OS, *Nafs* is the **scoring spine** of the system — the four canonical stations (Ammarah, Lawwamah, Mulhamah, Mutmainnah) are the dimensions of the meter.

### Nifaq (نفاق)
Hypocrisy — the difference between outward appearance and inward reality. One of the 200 negative attributes. The classical *munafiqun* are discussed at length in Surah Al-Baqarah.

---

## Q

### Qalb (قلب)
See *Al-Qalb*. The heart.

### Qanaah (قناعة)
Contentment with what one has. The third step of the *Envy → Contentment* pathway. The hadith: "Richness is not having many possessions; richness is the richness of the soul." (Sahih Muslim 1051)

### Qiblah (قبلة)
The direction of prayer (towards the Ka'bah in Makkah). Not used in Heart OS directly, but referenced in the *Prayer* habit category.

### Quran (قرآن)
The Book revealed to the Prophet ﷺ. The first source of Islamic law and the highest authority. Heart OS cites Quran for almost every recommendation.

---

## R

### Rahmah (رحمة)
Mercy, compassion. The terminal step of the *Anger → Mercy* pathway. One of the most-mentioned attributes of Allah — His names *Al-Rahman* (The Merciful) and *Al-Rahim* (The Compassionate) are recited in every prayer.

### Raja (رجاء)
Hope, especially hope in Allah's mercy. The third step of the *Sin → Love* pathway. Distinct from *khawf* (fear) — the believer balances both.

### Rifq (رفق)
Gentleness. The fourth step of the *Anger → Mercy* pathway. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah is gentle and loves gentleness in all matters." (Sahih al-Bukhari 6927)

### Riya (رياء)
Showing off, performing acts of worship to be seen by people. One of the 200 negative attributes. The Prophet ﷺ called it *shirk al-asghar* — the lesser shirk (Musnad Ahmad 23630).

### Ruh (روح)
The spirit. Distinct from *nafs* (the self). The ruh is the divine breath that Allah breathed into Adam (Surah As-Sajdah 32:9). Heart OS does not model the ruh separately — it focuses on the *nafs* and the *qalb*.

---

## S

### Sabr (صبر)
Patience, perseverance, restraint. The starting cure for Anger. One of the most-mentioned attributes in the Quran ("*innama yuwa al-sabirun ajrahum bi ghayri hisab*" — Surah Az-Zumar 39:10). Half of faith, the other half being *shukr* (gratitude).

### Sakinah (سكينه)
Tranquility, stillness. The terminal step of the *Anxiety → Peace* pathway. The Quran says *sakinah* descends upon the believers in remembrance (Surah Ar-Ra'd 13:28).

### Salah (صلاة)
The ritual prayer performed five times a day. Distinct from *dua* (supplication). Heart OS tracks *Salah* as a habit category, not as a content recommendation.

### Shukr (شكر)
Gratitude, thankfulness. The second step of the *Envy → Contentment* pathway. The Quran pairs *sabr* and *shukr* repeatedly (e.g. Surah Al-Baqarah 2:153).

### Sirat al-Mustaqim (الصراط المستقيم)
"The straight path." The path referred to in Surah Al-Fatiha. The 8 master pathways in Heart OS are concrete instantiations of *sirat al-mustaqim* for specific diseases.

### Sunnah (سنة)
The practice of the Prophet ﷺ. Distinct from *Hadith* (the textual record of the Sunnah). The Quran and Sunnah are the two primary sources of guidance in Heart OS.

## H · Hadith authentication

### Hadith authentication standard
The Quran, Hadith, and dua references in the HeartOS seed (`200-Attributes.xlsx`, `50-cores.xlsx`, `NafsMutmainna-200-Attributes.docx`) follow a consistent authentication standard:

| Collection | Authentication level | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Sahih al-Bukhari | Muttafaqun 'alayh | Cited for the most fundamental attributes; authenticated by scholarly consensus. |
| Sahih Muslim | Muttafaqun 'alayh | Cited extensively; authenticated by scholarly consensus. |
| Jami' at-Tirmidhi | Hasan or Sahih | Many entries are graded by At-Tirmidhi himself; the appendix marks some as (Hasan). |
| Sunan Abu Dawud | Mostly Sahih / Hasan | Cited where it is the canonical source for a particular hadith. |
| Sunan Ibn Majah | Mostly Sahih / Hasan | Cited for specific reports. |
| Musnad Ahmad | Mixed | Some hadith in Musnad Ahmad are Sahih, some Hasan, some Da'if. Cited only where the report is well-known. |
| Shuab al-Iman (Al-Bayhaqi) | Hasan | Cited in the docx for several attributes. |
| Sunan An-Nasa'i | Sahih / Hasan | Cited where it is the only source. |

### Authentication methodology
For v1.0, the references are preserved as authored in the source files. The user has marked the 200 Attributes and 50 Core Emotions tables as **authentic** per the seed. A future v1.1 revision may add a `Hadith_Grade` column to `attributes` and `emotions` so each reference is explicitly tagged Sahih, Hasan, or Da'if per the grading of a chosen muhaddith (e.g. Al-Albani's Sahih and Da'if collections).

### Scholarly attribution
Where classical Islamic scholarship is cited (e.g. Ibn al-Qayyim's Madarij al-Salikin, Imam Al-Ghazali's Ihya Ulum al-Din, Ibn Hajar al-Asqalani's Fath al-Bari, Imam An-Nawawi's Riyad as-Salihin), the attributions are to the recognised works of those scholars. These are accepted as authentic Islamic sources per the project's content guidelines.

### Quranic references
Every Quran reference in the seed has been verified to point to an actual verse in the Mushaf. The references use the standard format `Surah Name Surah:Ayayh` (e.g. "Al-Baqarah 2:153"). Translations are from the recognised English translations (Sahih International, Pickthall, Yusuf Ali) and Urdu translations (Maulana Maududi, Fateh Muhammad Jalandhari).

## T

### Tahajjud (تهجد)
The night prayer, performed after sleeping and before Fajr. One of the most spiritually powerful voluntary acts. Recommended in several Heart OS pathways as a deepening practice.

### Tawadu (تواضع)
Humility, lowliness. The second step of the *Pride → Humility* pathway. The opposite of *kibr*.

### Tawakkul (توكل)
Reliance on Allah. The starting cure for Anxiety. The Quran commands the believers to *put their trust in Allah* (Surah At-Talaq 65:3). Distinct from passivity — the hadith says: "Tie your camel, then put your trust in Allah." (Sunan al-Tirmidhi 2517)

### Tawbah (توبة)
Repentance. The starting point of the *Sin → Love* pathway. The Quran says Allah loves the *tawwabin* (the repentant) (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:222).

### Tazkiyah (تزكية)
Purification of the soul. The classical Islamic discipline of *tazkiyat al-nafs* — the very thing Heart OS is designed to support. Mentioned in Surah Ash-Shams 91:9 alongside the failure of *tazkiyah*.

---

## U

### Ujb (عجب)
Self-admiration, being impressed with one's own achievements. One of the 200 negative attributes. The Quran warns: "Do not claim yourselves to be pure." (Surah An-Najm 53:32)

### Ulema (علماء)
The scholars. The qualified Islamic scholars. Heart OS does not replace the ulema — for specific fiqh questions, the user should consult one.

### Umrah (عمرة)
The lesser pilgrimage to Makkah. Not specifically modelled in Heart OS but referenced in the *Worship* domain.

### Usul (أصول)
"Roots" — the foundational sources of Islamic law. The *usul al-fiqh* are: Quran, Sunnah, Ijma' (consensus), Qiyas (analogy). Heart OS draws from the first two only.

---

## W

### Wilayah (ولاية)
Divine *closeness* — Allah's special protection of a believer. The terminal step of the *Knowledge → Nearness* pathway. The Quran says: "Allah is the Wali (protector) of those who believe." (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:257)

### Wudu (وضوء)
The ritual ablution performed before prayer. The Prophetic prescription for the onset of anger (Sahih al-Bukhari 6116). Recommended as the *Daily Action* for the emotion Anger.

---

## Y

### Yaqeen (يقين)
Certainty, conviction. The second step of the *Anxiety → Peace* pathway and the second step of the *Knowledge → Nearness* pathway. The Quran places *yaqeen* in three states: *ilm al-yaqeen* (knowledge of certainty), *'ayn al-yaqeen* (eye of certainty), and *haqq al-yaqeen* (truth of certainty) (Surah At-Takathur 102:5-7).

### Yaqzah (يقظة)
Spiritual awakening — the moment one *notices* one's heedlessness. The starting point of the *Heedlessness → Presence* pathway.

---

## Z

### Zuhd (زهد)
Asceticism, renunciation of attachment to the material world. The second step of the *Dunya → Zuhd* pathway. *Zuhd* is not *poverty* — it is *freedom from attachment*. The classical definition: "Zuhd in the dunya is not to forbid yourself the lawful; it is to forbid the lawful from occupying your heart." (Ibn al-Qayyim)
